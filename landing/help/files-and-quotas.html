Files and quotas

Two places your work lives

In your browser: the editor keeps your open project and a local media cache on your machine. Local work never touches the relay, and you can edit signed out for as long as you like.

On the relay: when you save to the cloud, queue a job or publish media for a cloud project, files go to storage space that belongs to your account and only your account. Other accounts cannot list, read or write that space. The relay is a hosted database and file store (Supabase) that the CutLab server and your browser both talk to.

The limits during beta

LimitValueHow it is enforced
Working storage 200 MB per account A database check counts your files when a job is created, and the server checks again before it starts a job. A job that would cross the limit is refused with the reason.
Single file 50 MB The storage bucket has its own platform limit at the same number, so an oversize upload fails at the door even if a client tries to send one.

The relay runs on a shared free tier, 1 GB of storage and 5 GB of monthly transfer for every account together. The limits keep one account from crowding out the rest while the beta is small.

What counts toward the 200 MB

  • Originals you upload as inputs for server jobs, and the outputs those jobs write back, including finished renders.
  • Media the server publishes for your cloud projects: the original plus its proxy, thumbnail and waveform data.
  • Inputs and outputs of agent renders, while they sit in your space between upload and download.

Project documents themselves are small JSON and live in the database, outside the storage meter.

The meter

Open Account in the editor's top bar. The storage bar there adds up the real files in your relay space at the moment you open it, which is the same set of files the quota counts. It also lists your cloud projects with their saved version counts and your recent jobs on both render lanes.

Working within the limits

  • Download finished renders promptly. Outputs stay in your space until you remove the account, and they are usually the biggest files there.
  • Trim long source clips before uploading. A job only needs the range the edit uses.
  • Keep masters on your own disk. CutLab's relay is working space for jobs in flight, and your data export covers project documents and records, not a backup of every original.

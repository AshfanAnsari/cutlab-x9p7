Connect your AI

A connect token is a personal key that starts with clt_. It lets an AI assistant work on your CutLab projects: reading a project, running prompt edits, and rendering jobs you queue for it. You connect your chat, never an API key, so nothing about the connection touches an AI company's billing.

Create a token

  1. Open the editor and sign in. The token registers under your account, so sign-in comes first.
  2. Open the View menu and choose Settings.
  3. Scroll to the Connections section.
  4. Choose Generate new token. Signed in, the token's hash registers under your account in the same step.
  5. Choose Verify. The badge should read Connected.

You can also paste a token an assistant issued you into the same field and verify it the same way.

Hand it to your Muse

Give your Muse the token and point it at the agent brief, a short document that explains the whole protocol: 

https://hjdyvaetxhvxzgsdlibt.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/cutlab-public/AGENT-BRIEF.md

The brief covers the connector endpoint, the claim and render flow, and the honesty rules an agent follows while it holds one of your jobs. Your Muse can also fetch the renderer package itself from the same public host when a render needs it.

What the token can do

  • List and read your projects.
  • Fetch time-limited download links for the media of your jobs.
  • See, claim, complete or fail render jobs you queued for your agent.
  • Drive prompt edits on your projects through the prompt lane.

Every query the connector answers is scoped to the account that registered the token. There is no setting that widens it, and the relay stores only a hash of the token, never the token itself.

Stay safe with it

  • Treat the token like a password. Anyone holding it can act on your projects as your agent.
  • Revoke it in the same Connections section whenever you stop using an assistant. The relay refuses a revoked token from that moment.
  • If a token was registered before you had an account, sign in and register it again so it is scoped to you.

One limit to know: only an assistant that can run code on a machine can render for you. A chat window that only talks can still read projects and help with prompt edits. The rendering flow is covered in Render with your Muse.
